When to fill up in South Africa: how the monthly fuel price cycle works

Fuel is one of the few household costs in South Africa that changes on a published schedule. If you know how the cycle works, you can decide whether to fill up now or wait, instead of guessing at the pump. This guide explains the cycle in plain terms and gives you a simple rule and the maths behind it.

How fuel prices are set

  • Petrol has a regulated retail price. It is adjusted once a month, and the change usually takes effect on the first Wednesday of the month.
  • The adjustment mostly follows two things: international fuel product prices and the rand/US dollar exchange rate. Taxes and levies, such as the general fuel levy and the Road Accident Fund levy, are added on top.
  • Diesel has a regulated wholesale price, but the retail price is not fixed. It can differ from one station to the next, so it is worth comparing.
  • Prices differ between coastal and inland zones, mainly because of the cost of moving fuel inland.

Reading the mid-month signal

During the month, the Central Energy Fund publishes daily data showing whether fuel is being over- or under-recovered at current prices. Motoring and industry groups and the news summarise this as an "expected increase" or "expected decrease" for the next month.

Treat this as a signal, not a promise. The number can move a lot in the last week of the month, and the official adjustment is only announced shortly before it takes effect.

A simple fill-up rule

Late-month signalHousehold move
Clear increase expectedFill up before the change date, if you have the cash and would use the fuel anyway.
Clear decrease expectedBuy only what you need until the change date, then fill up.
Flat or unclearFill up as normal. Waiting will not gain you anything worth the trouble.

The tank maths

The rand effect is simple: litres × change per litre.

Example: a 50-litre tank and an expected increase of R1.00 a litre. Filling before the change saves about 50 × R1.00 = R50 on that tank. On a 70-litre bakkie tank it is about R70.

Compare that with what it costs to get the saving. A 20 km detour to a cheaper station, or a late-night trip, can cost more than it saves.

Other levers that don't depend on the date

  • Combine errands into one trip on the days you already drive.
  • Check tyre pressure monthly against the figure on your door sticker or in your manual.
  • Take extra weight out of the boot.
  • For diesel, compare a few stations on your usual route, since retail prices vary.

Safety first: fill up in daylight at a busy forecourt, and don't go hunting a few cents across quiet areas at night.

FAQ

When do fuel prices change in South Africa?

Petrol price adjustments usually take effect on the first Wednesday of each month. The official figure is announced shortly before that date.

Why is diesel a different price at different stations?

Only the wholesale diesel price is regulated. Retail diesel prices are set by each outlet, so they can differ.

Can Judgment OS predict next month's fuel price?

No. It reads published signals, such as the mid-month over- and under-recovery data. The official announcement decides the actual price.

Is filling up early always worth it?

Only when a clear increase is expected, you have the cash, and you would use the fuel anyway. The saving is litres times the change per litre, so a small change on a small tank is worth little.
