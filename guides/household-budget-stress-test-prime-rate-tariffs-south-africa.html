Household budget stress test for South Africa: prime rate, Eskom and municipal increases

Three big household costs in South Africa change on dates you can see coming: interest rates, electricity tariffs and municipal charges. A stress test takes an hour. You work out what each change would cost your household before it arrives, and decide now where the money will come from.

The three dates to put in your calendar

WhatWhenWhat it moves
SARB Monetary Policy Committee decisionSix meetings a year (usually January, March, May, July, September, November)The repo rate, and with it the prime lending rate
Eskom tariff changeUsually 1 April for direct Eskom customersElectricity bought directly from Eskom
Municipal tariffsUsually 1 July (start of the municipal financial year)Municipal electricity, water, refuse and rates

The prime lending rate is normally the repo rate plus 3.5 percentage points. So a 0.25-point repo change usually means a 0.25-point prime change.

Step 1: list what is linked to prime

Go through your statements: home loan, vehicle finance, personal loans, credit cards and overdraft. For each one, write the balance, the rate (for example "prime + 0.5%" or a fixed rate) and the months left. Fixed-rate debt won't move when prime does. Your contract says which kind you have.

Step 2: run the rate maths

Example only: R1,000,000 home loan, 20 years left.

At 10.50%: about R9,984 a month. At 10.75%: about R10,152 a month. That is roughly R168 more a month for each 0.25-point rise on this loan.

Your bank's figure is the real one. Most banking apps show your new instalment soon after a rate change, and you can ask your bank for it.

Step 3: run the electricity and municipal maths

  • Take your average monthly kWh from the last three bills or prepaid slips.
  • Multiply it by the new tariff once it is published. Until then, as a rough guide, multiply your current bill by (1 + the approved percentage increase).
  • Do the same for water, refuse and rates using your municipal statement.

Step 4: decide where the money comes from

  1. Add up the monthly increases from steps 2 and 3.
  2. Mark which ones are confirmed and which are only possible.
  3. Pick one line in your budget to cut, or one buffer to draw on, for each confirmed increase.
  4. Write the date you will check again: the next MPC date or tariff date.

FAQ

How is the prime rate set in South Africa?

Banks normally set prime at the SARB repo rate plus 3.5 percentage points, so it moves when the Monetary Policy Committee changes the repo rate.

When do electricity prices go up?

Eskom tariff changes for direct customers usually take effect on 1 April. Municipal electricity tariffs usually change on 1 July.

How much does a 0.25% rate rise cost?

It depends on your balance, rate and term. For example, on a R1,000,000 bond with 20 years left at 10.50%, a 0.25-point rise adds about R168 a month.

Does Judgment OS know my bank balance?

No. You enter your own numbers, and the budget board keeps them in your browser on your phone.
