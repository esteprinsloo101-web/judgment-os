Family monthly budget in South Africa: school runs, multi-stop trips and one shared plan

A family budget breaks in the places a single-person budget doesn't: two or three school runs, sports and extra lessons, a grocery shop split over several stops, and more than one person spending. This guide builds a family monthly budget the whole household can follow, with the transport maths most budgets skip.

Step 1: start with take-home, not salary

Write down what actually lands in the bank each month for every earner, after tax, UIF, medical aid and pension deductions. Note the pay date for each one. If one salary lands on the 25th and another on the 1st, your month has two starts.

Step 2: fixed costs first

  • Rent or bond, and levies
  • Electricity, water and data
  • Insurance, medical aid gap and funeral cover
  • Debt repayments
  • School fees, transport and aftercare

Subtract these from take-home. What is left is what you have to live on for food, fuel and everything else.

Step 3: the transport maths

Most families underestimate fuel because trips are spread over the week. Work it out once:

Weekly litres = kilometres per week × your car's litres per 100 km ÷ 100

Example only: school runs, work and one shopping trip add up to 180 km a week. The car uses 8 L/100 km, so 180 × 8 ÷ 100 = 14.4 litres a week. At R26 a litre that is about R374 a week, or around R1,600 a month.

Then look for trips to combine. A lift club for one school run, or doing the grocery stops on the way home instead of as a separate trip, shows up straight away in these numbers.

Step 4: envelopes per person and per job

Give each spending area a monthly amount: groceries, fuel, kids' activities, household, and a small personal amount for each adult. When an envelope runs out, the family decides together whether to move money from another one. That way one person isn't left policing everyone else.

Step 5: plan for the known spikes

  • January: school fees, uniforms and stationery arrive together.
  • Fuel changes: monthly, usually on the first Wednesday.
  • Tariffs: Eskom usually changes in April, municipalities in July.
  • December: travel and gifts, followed by a long January.

Put aside a small amount each month for these instead of meeting them all at once.

Step 6: a ten-minute weekly check-in

  1. How much is left in each envelope?
  2. What is coming next week: trips, fees, birthdays?
  3. One decision: what to cut, move or buy early.

FAQ

How do I start a family budget in South Africa?

Start from each earner's take-home pay and pay date, subtract fixed costs such as housing, school, insurance and debt, then split what is left into envelopes for groceries, fuel, kids and household.

How do I work out a monthly fuel budget for school runs?

Multiply the kilometres you drive each week by your car's litres per 100 km, divide by 100, and multiply by the price per litre. Multiply the weekly total by about 4.3 for a month.

When do family costs spike in South Africa?

January (school fees and uniforms), monthly fuel changes, Eskom tariffs usually in April, municipal tariffs usually in July, and December travel.
